If you're building anything that involves AI, then you have to know about the bitter lesson. The bitter lesson is an article by Rich Sutton from March 13th, 2019. And the best quote from it is also the first sentence. The biggest lesson that can be read from 70 years of AI research is that general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective and by a large margin. In other words, as the cost per unit of computation falls, in other words, as computation gets cheaper, as computer power gets cheaper, you can throw more computing power at the problem and it will generally outperform any optimizations that you've done on top. Most AI research has been conducted as if the computation available to the agent were constant. In other words, we're behaving as if the ground we're standing on is solid, but it's not. As computers get more powerful for cheaper, it doesn't matter if you found this crazy new technique for improving your AI app. Eventually, the computers will get so powerful, the AI will get so powerful that will outperform your solution and your optimization. Well, that was probably just a waste of time that you could have just spent waiting for the AI to get better. That is the bitter lesson that nothing really matters in AI research apart from just improving the power of these machines. Now, this is controversial, I would say. If you see someone on the internet talking about the bitter lesson, this is the lesson they're talking about. So, what do you think? Is it worth it to optimize our applications or are we just going to get beaten by computation?
