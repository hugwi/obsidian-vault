September 7, 2026 Labor Day has long recognized the contributions of workers to the economy. With AI agents increasingly part of the workforce, what will human contribution look like?

As AI takes rote tasks off employees’ plates, the work that remains can require greater judgment and critical thinking—and be more cognitively demanding. “The rise of AI doesn’t mean we all suddenly delegate our responsibilities for quality control and critical thinking,” says [Kate Smaje](https://www.mckinsey.com/our-people/kate-smaje), McKinsey senior partner and global leader of technology and AI, in a [recent episode](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/your-ai-agents-need-performance-management-too) of [*McKinsey Talks Talent*](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organization/mckinsey-talks-talent-podcast). “There’s a premium on those capabilities now.”

Smaje, coauthor of the recent book [*Rewired*](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/mckinsey-on-books/rewired), joins Senior Partner [Brooke Weddle](https://www.mckinsey.com/our-people/brooke-weddle) and Partner [Bryan Hancock](https://www.mckinsey.com/our-people/bryan-hancock) to explore what it takes to manage a workforce of people and AI agents—from reimagining roles and workflows to managing the performance of digital workers.

September 6, 2026 Across industries, AI is changing how companies operate, make decisions, and grow. Through a series of podcast conversations, Senior Partner [Eric Kutcher](https://www.mckinsey.com/our-people/eric-kutcher) talks with CEOs about how they are navigating that shift and connecting AI to the broader priorities of their businesses.

At Invesco, President and CEO Andrew Schlossberg is taking both a top-down and bottom-up approach to AI. The company has put the tools in every employee’s hands, [allowing ideas to emerge from people rethinking how they work](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/earning-and-sustaining-trust-in-the-age-of-ai). The leadership challenge is finding the right balance. “You have to resist the urge to make it completely top down and controlled,” Schlossberg says, “and also resist the urge to make it completely bottom up and entrepreneurial.”

At Snowflake, CEO Sridhar Ramaswamy sees [AI changing who can create software](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/ai-is-turning-every-company-into-a-software-company) —not just how quickly it gets built. “One prediction is that many more people will be able to write software,” he says, raising broader questions about how leaders redeploy people and rethink work as those capabilities spread.

And as AI takes on more work behind the screen, it can create more room for work away from it. In real estate, Hines co-CEO Laura Hines-Pierce wants AI to “ [free people from their computers](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/real-estate/our-insights/the-new-advantage-in-the-age-of-ai-building-in-the-real-world) and enable them to spend more time creating value in the real world”—building relationships, sourcing deals, overseeing development projects, and delivering experiences to occupants.

Listen to these podcasts for more perspectives on how leaders are [meeting the AI moment](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/how-the-best-ceos-are-meeting-the-ai-moment) and embedding it into how their organizations operate, make decisions, and grow.

August 22, 2026 As a new school year gets underway in many parts of the world, students are preparing for a future shaped by AI. But succeeding will take more than technical know-how. As AI transforms how work gets done, distinctly human skills—like clear, confident communication—matter more than ever. Yet high-quality communication coaching remains out of reach for many.

JA Worldwide, a nonprofit that prepares young people for entrepreneurship, work readiness, and financial health, partnered with McKinsey to change that. Through the firm’s AI for Social Good program and QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey, the team developed JA Pitch Master, a gen-AI-powered communication coach [that helps young people around the world refine pitch presentations](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/education/how-we-help-clients/preparing-the-next-generation-of-confident-communicators-in-an-ai-driven-economy) through personalized, practical feedback.

The tool demonstrates how AI can be used to complement and strengthen the human skills that are increasingly valuable in the workplace. “When thoughtfully designed and responsibly deployed,” says Stacie Fieth, vice president of education at JA Worldwide, “generative AI can fundamentally strengthen how young people practice, reflect, and grow.”

This back-to-school season, explore more McKinsey insights on improving education at scale and expanding opportunities for young learners to build bright futures.
