# China Built a Chip Up to 478x Faster Than an Nvidia GPU

![China Built a Chip Up to 478x Faster Than an Nvidia GPU](https://www.youtube.com/embed/q4DgFp9RAfo)

## Description

Chinese researchers have developed a specialized computing chip based on phase-change memristors that can perform certain neural-dynamics calculations dramatically faster and with lower power consumption than conventional digital hardware.

The project was led by researchers from Peking University together with the Shanghai Institute of Microsystem and Information Technology at the Chinese Academy of Sciences. The results were published in Science.

The experimental chip uses phase-change memristors to combine data storage and computation directly inside the same physical devices. This compute-in-memory approach reduces the constant movement of information between memory and processors—one of the major sources of latency and energy consumption in conventional computing architectures.

The chip was fabricated using a 40-nanometer process. Its compute-in-memory and conductance-drift arrays occupy only 0.28 mm², while the system operates at a clock frequency of 50 MHz. Despite those relatively modest specifications, it completed a single neural dynamical system iteration in just 2.12 milliseconds.

Compared with state-of-the-art dedicated NDS accelerators, the researchers measured a 3.82x to 36.27x increase in speed while reducing power consumption by 11.75x to 24.73x.

The largest advantage appeared in specialized applications such as cortical-surface reconstruction and three-dimensional manifold modeling. In these tasks, the complete NDS system was reported to outperform an Nvidia A100 GPU by between 50.38x and 478.18x.

That does not mean the chip is hundreds of times faster than an Nvidia GPU in gaming, large language models or general AI workloads. It is a specialized accelerator designed around neural dynamical systems.

One unusual part of the design is that researchers turned a traditional weakness of phase-change memory—conductance drift—into a computational feature. The physical evolution of the memory devices helps perform adaptive numerical calculations directly inside the hardware.

The technology could eventually support real-time brain modeling, medical imaging, brain-computer interfaces, neural navigation, physical simulations and other applications where complex dynamical systems must be calculated with very low latency.
