How I'm using Notebook LM and why it's become a great companion to my thinking in Obsidian. Create a notebook for each topic you're investigating. One topic per notebook. On the left are your sources, in the middle you chat with them, and then on the right you create outputs like mind maps, podcasts, and slide decks. So once you add your sources, your PDFs, your YouTube videos, your websites, you can start asking questions about them. Every answer has citations that link back to the exact spot in your source. And the context window is massive, up to 25 million words. You can toggle sources on and off, and here's a hidden gem. You can give Notebook LM a custom personality. Tell it to act like a marketing expert, a friendly teacher, whatever fits your research. It'll tailor every response to that role. So once I've gotten the terrain, the general shape of a topic, that's where Obsidian comes in. I'll manually transfer my best insights, and then actually retire this notebook. Notebook LM gets me oriented fast, but Obsidian is where my idea verse actually lives.
