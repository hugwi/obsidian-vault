## Transcript

Pretty much everyone can get, um, better bang for your buck by using a lower effort level. Lower effort basically means fewer tokens spent on exploration. So, the agent is more efficient as it goes through your code base. Exploration is a task that you don't really need effort for. As you crank up the effort, the agent is going to be spending more thinking tokens to do the same work, more reasoning tokens. And what that means is that it's going to push harder on things that you don't need to push on. Everyone looks at the charts for the benchmarks that models use and they see that, okay, if you crank it up to max effort, you're going to get higher quality outputs. The weird thing about those charts is that they don't really take into account the crucial thing, which is that the tasks stay static, whereas you have the opportunity as the developer to change the way that you specify tasks to make things easier for the agent. The more high-quality information you give it, the lower effort you actually need to complete the task. So, I recommend actually starting, if you're using Opus or Fable or something, starting with the lowest effort you have access to and then cranking it up from there. You do still need a little bit of thinking tokens just because chain of thought is such a useful way of working with agents and getting it to do good stuff, but honestly, just try a lower effort level. You'll be surprised by what you get.
