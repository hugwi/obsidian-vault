## The Problem With AI Skin

I’ve spent hours tweaking prompts, trying to get Midjourney to produce faces that don’t look like they’ve been Photoshopped into oblivion.

I’d get an incredible image, but the skin?

Too perfect.

The key isn’t in the software. It’s in the words you use for your prompt.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:2912/format:webp/1*7TauvA4DB69kxdtkJf4mow.png)

Prompt: A portrait of a 42-year-old man from London, natural skin, unretouched, slight skin imperfection, a bit overweight, baggy eyes, looking straight to camera, cinematic, high key, every day photo on a plain white studio background — v 6.1 — style raw — ar 16:9

## Testing Different Words to See What Works

Midjourney is so powerful, I’m not sure it’s the tool that’s at fault (Garbage in, garbage out right?)

So how do you get images to look really good.

Realism is one way.

I’m often asked how I get Midjourney photos to appear realistic.

There are a few simple techniques, like the genre of image, or keywords that hint at it.

But you can get really specific too.

I got so fed up that I decided to create myself a visual reference sheet. Here it is:

## But First, a Base Prompt

Let’s start with a base prompt:

> *A portrait of a woman in her 40s with short windswept red hair, looking straight to camera, cinematic, high key, every day photo on a plain white studio background — v 6.1 — style raw — ar 16:9*

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:2912/format:webp/1*SIxAXTXw_dgcCuzQT0DOqQ.png)

We’ve not really done anything yet, sit tight

We’ve got a few things going on already, like the studio setting lending a professional look to it.

With Midjourney 6.1 skin has by default become a bit more natural, and the high key light and studio environment will already be pushing us in this direction.

After some digging around on Reddit and my own extensive testing I’ve come up with a few phrases that might help your Midjourney photography.

In each case only the one phrase was added. Nothing else was changed about the prompt.

I also included it near the front, like this:  
“A portrait of a woman in her 40s, \[PHRASE\], \[rest of prompt\]”
